How a meeting moves from turn to turn

Workflow overview

The meeting engine keeps track of who should speak next, whether turns should be balanced, and how the conversation moves through each cycle. This page explains the behavior you will see in the GUI so the settings make sense before you press start.

Context management

Each turn carries forward the meeting state that matters for the next speaker: who already spoke, which cycle the conversation is in, and what the current route or proposal looks like. That shared context is what keeps the meeting coherent as it moves from one turn to the next.

When balanced turns are on, the engine tries to preserve a full cycle before anyone repeats. When balanced turns are off, the flow becomes freer and the engine can move without waiting for a complete pass through the participants.

What happens during a meeting

Plan the order

Before the first turn, the app decides which participant should speak first and whether the list should be treated as a balanced cycle.

Run the discussion

Each participant speaks when it is their turn, and the engine records the result so the next turn can be selected correctly.

Refine the route

The meeting can refine the plan after discussion, which is useful when the group needs to adjust the route or improve the proposal.

Review the result

The final state is kept in the logs and meeting data so you can review what happened after the session ends.

Turn rules in plain English

Setting What it means
Round robin Everyone gets a turn in order, so balanced turns are always on.
Random The next speaker is chosen randomly. You can keep balance on for one pass per cycle, or turn it off for a freer order.
Inviting The meeting can invite a particular speaker or move more deliberately when you want to guide the discussion.
Facilitating The engine helps structure the conversation while still letting the meeting progress naturally.

Balanced turns and cycles

A cycle is the span in which each participant gets one turn. When balanced turns are enabled, the engine tries to complete that cycle before repeating anyone. When balanced turns are off, the engine can pick the next speaker more freely and the idea of a strict cycle is no longer useful.

That is why round robin keeps balanced turns on automatically, while random mode can be used either way depending on whether you want a full cycle or a freer sequence.

How the settings in the GUI affect the flow

  1. Pick the turn rule first.
  2. Leave balanced turns on for predictable rotation, especially for round robin.
  3. Turn it off only when you want a looser order, such as a fully random run.
  4. Check the log viewer after starting the meeting so you can confirm the selected behavior matches what you expected.
