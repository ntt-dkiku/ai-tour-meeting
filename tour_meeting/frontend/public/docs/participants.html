Participant

This page describes how a participant is defined and how it acts during a meeting.

Persona

Each LLM agent (participant) is instantiated with a distinct persona. The persona is given to the LLM as a system prompt, and the participant takes actions during the meeting while role-playing according to it. The persona consists of the following attributes:

Name

The participant's name.

Background

The participant's experience or situation.

Personality

The participant's stable traits.

Preferences

The participant's preferences related to the tour.

Personal goals

The participant's personal goals in the tour. Each participant advances the discussion in pursuit of their own personal goals while remaining aligned with the global goals of the meeting.

Role

The participant's role in the meeting: facilitator or attendee.

Speaking style

The participant's speaking tone, such as formal or friendly.

Explanation style

A strategy for convincing others to accept their proposal: subjective, contrastive, or both. The former provides subjective opinions of the proposal based on the participant's preferences. The latter explains the objective pros and cons by comparing the proposed itinerary with the current one in terms of objective metrics such as cost, time, and number of destinations.

LLM settings

You can select an LLM that instantiates each participant, together with model parameters such as temperature and seed. Both commercial LLMs and local LLMs (via Ollama or vLLM) are supported — see Quick start for how to serve local models. Context management methods, including auto-compaction and history truncation, can also be configured individually for each participant (see Context management).

Actions

The meeting proceeds in a turn-based manner, and each participant may take multiple actions during their single turn. There are two types of actions: intermediate and terminal. Intermediate actions may be taken multiple times within a turn, whereas once a terminal action is taken, the current turn ends.

The intermediate actions are common to both phases:

search

Issues a query to retrieve information, such as the location or cost of destinations, via web search.

ask

Asks another participant a question and receives a response.

reflect

Organizes and reviews the discussion conducted so far.

The terminal actions differ between the conversation phase and the voting phase. In the conversation phase, they include:

propose

Proposes a new itinerary. This triggers the voting phase.

satisfied

Indicates that the participant is satisfied with the current itinerary.

pass

Ends the turn without expressing a specific position (available when the volunteer option is enabled).

In the voting phase, they include:

accept

Votes for the proposed itinerary.

reject

Votes against the proposed itinerary.

score

Assigns a score to the proposed itinerary (used with score-based voting rules).

See Meeting workflow for how these actions drive the meeting between the two phases.

Human participant (interactive mode)

AI Tour Meeting also allows human users to participate in meetings. At their turn, they can interact with the LLM participants through the chat box by executing the same actions as the LLM participants. When proposing an itinerary, they can manually edit the destinations, or click Generate with AI to have an LLM refine their proposal interactively.

A typical use case of this feature is to represent friends who are unable to attend the meeting as LLM participants based on their personas. By discussing the itinerary with these agents, you can incorporate the absent participants' perspectives into the tour planning.
